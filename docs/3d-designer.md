# Standalone 3D Designer

The project workspace's **3D Designer** stores one ground-floor model per project. It does not use Documents, drawing revisions, pages, OCR, or Plans calibration. Geometry is saved in metres as double-precision values; metric/imperial switching changes display and entry only. Top View and 3D View render the same wall coordinates.

Choose **Wall**, drag from the start to the end on the grid, then enter exact length, height, thickness, and alignment. The dragged direction and start point determine placement; typed length determines the final endpoint. The path is the wall centreline for **Centreline**. For **Inside face**, the centreline is offset half the thickness to the path's left; **Outside face** offsets it to the right. Face A is the left side of the start-to-end direction and Face B the right. Grid snapping is 0.25 m. Nearby wall endpoints and centre lines snap before the grid. Horizontal/vertical lock constrains the end point; Free allows any angle.

Choose **Door** or **Window** and click a wall, or drag its toolbox item onto a wall in Top View. The distance field measures from the wall's start endpoint to the opening's **near edge**. Doors start at floor level. Openings are stored on one host wall and appear as physical voids through its thickness. Their positions follow edits to the host wall unless the edit would place them outside the wall, in which case the edit is rejected.

Each wall has one stable ID, including shared walls between rooms. Face A and Face B have independent room labels, plastering, painting, and finish heights. Reveals around openings are excluded from finish quantities. The backend calculates quantities from the canonical model; the frontend preview follows the same rules. A 6 m × 3 m × 0.2 m wall with a 1 m × 2 m floor-level door has 18 m² gross area, 16 m² net brickwork area, 3.2 m³ net volume, and 32 m² of finish area when both faces are fully finished.

In Top View or 3D View, dragging a new wall endpoint onto an existing wall within a 14 px screen-space radius previews an intentional join. A square T-junction keeps the through wall continuous; a square corner keeps the existing wall continuous. The adjoining wall trims by half the continuous wall's thickness at that end. The wall dialog rechecks the join after exact dimensions and alignment are entered. Ambiguous targets require an explicit choice; unsupported angles and contacts lost by editing require correction or an explicit unconnected placement. Escape cancels a drag. Select and drag an existing wall to move it, or edit its start coordinates and dimensions in Properties. Its hosted openings and joins are revalidated before the edit is accepted.

The compact Connections section shows joined walls, offers Show and Disconnect, and keeps manual controls under Advanced connections. Disconnecting does not automatically reconnect on render. Existing saved models remain unchanged when opened. Each geometry-and-join placement is one undoable action. Junction trim is shown separately as an area deduction and removes buried end regions from both wall-face finish totals. Non-square junctions, undeclared intersections, overlapping walls, and cyclic trim dependencies remain provisional and cannot be marked Reviewed. The editor does not silently merge walls.

Saving sends one validated snapshot with a model version. A stale version is rejected. Save and review changes use transactions and write audit events. Quantity-changing saves return Reviewed models to Draft. Authentication, ADMIN permissions, CSRF, and active project/client checks follow the existing API rules.

Before applying it to the dedicated local development database, inspection found **two old Building Model records with no walls, openings, or junctions**. The two empty records were removed. No Plans, Document, estimate, or other business tables were changed. Do not apply this destructive migration to a shared or production database without inspecting and exporting any old Building Model data there first.
